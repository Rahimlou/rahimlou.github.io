Genome-scale Phylogenomics of Fusarium oxysporum

Understanding evolutionary relationships within the Fusarium oxysporum species complex is challenging because of its extensive genomic diversity and repeated emergence of pathogenic lineages. To resolve these relationships, we reconstructed a genome-scale phylogeny using hundreds of conserved BUSCO genes across a comprehensive collection of genomes.

Dataset Summary
  • 572 genomes
  • 293 conserved BUSCO genes
  • 330,583 aligned nucleotide positions
  • Maximum-likelihood phylogenomic reconstruction

Key Findings

The genome-scale phylogeny recovered the major evolutionary lineages within the F. oxysporum species complex, including lineages 1–5 and the multiple sublineages of lineages 2 and 3. The extensive taxon sampling provides one of the largest phylogenomic frameworks currently available for this species complex.

An interesting observation concerns Lineage 3A. In the BUSCO phylogeny, Lineage 3A appears as a basal branch relative to the remaining members of lineages 2 and 3. However, this placement differs from our independent analyses using a 41-gene phylogenomic dataset and whole-genome Mash distance analysis, both of which consistently group Lineage 3A with the other members of Lineage 3.

This discordance suggests that the evolutionary history of Lineage 3A may be more complex than implied by a single phylogenomic approach. Possible explanations include incomplete lineage sorting, historical recombination, horizontal chromosome transfer, or differences in the phylogenetic signal captured by conserved BUSCO genes versus genome-wide similarity.

Ongoing Research

We are currently investigating the evolutionary position of Lineage 3A using complementary phylogenomic approaches, including expanded marker sets, genome-wide similarity analyses, and accessory genome comparisons to better understand the diversification of pathogenic lineages within the Fusarium oxysporum species complex.
