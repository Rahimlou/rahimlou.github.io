Fusarium Research

Comparative Genomics • Molecular Diagnostics • Evolutionary Biology

My Fusarium research integrates comparative genomics, phylogenomics, molecular diagnostics, and population genomics to better understand the evolution, diversity, and pathogenicity of economically important Fusarium species. A major focus is the development of genome-informed diagnostic assays for rapid detection and discrimination of pathogenic lineages.

Research Themes

Comparative Genomics

Genome-wide comparisons to investigate chromosome evolution, gene family expansion, accessory genomes, and lineage diversification.

Molecular Diagnostics

Development of race-specific genomic markers, PCR assays, e-probes, and diagnostic pipelines for Fusarium pathogens.

Phylogenomics

Reconstructing evolutionary relationships using genome-scale datasets to resolve species boundaries and evolutionary history.

Pathogenicity

Identification of candidate effectors, pathogenicity genes, horizontal chromosome transfer, and host specialization.

Population Genomics

Exploring genomic diversity among pathogenic populations, host-associated lineages, and global collections.

Bioinformatics

Development of reproducible workflows for genome assembly, annotation, comparative analyses, and diagnostic marker discovery.

Current Projects

Race-Specific Diagnostics for Fusarium oxysporum f. sp. lycopersici

Developing highly specific genome-derived markers capable of differentiating Fol Races 1, 2, and 3 across multiple evolutionary lineages using comparative genomics and in silico validation.

Comparative Genomics of Fusarium

Large-scale comparative analyses of publicly available genomes to investigate accessory chromosomes, structural variation, and evolutionary innovation.

Genome-based Marker Discovery Pipeline

Designing automated workflows for identifying diagnostic regions, primer design, e-probe generation, and laboratory validation.

Research Goals

Our long-term objective is to develop robust genome-informed diagnostic tools for economically important Fusarium pathogens while improving our understanding of their evolution, host adaptation, and mechanisms of pathogenicity through comparative and evolutionary genomics.
